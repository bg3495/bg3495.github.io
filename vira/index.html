01 — OVERVIEW

Recover the evidence, then reason.

Video MLLMs are strong on clean footage but stumble when blur, haze, low light, or occlusion hides the frames that matter. ViRA treats recovery as an action taken by the model itself, guided by the question.

Overview of ViRA. (a) Direct MLLM inference on a degraded video fails when task-relevant evidence is obscured. (b) ViRA diagnoses insufficient evidence, selects restoration tools, and grounds each call to the affected frames. (c) The recovered video restores the missing evidence while leaving already-sufficient frames untouched. Click to enlarge.

Abstract

Video understanding models perform well on clean, high-quality inputs, yet their robustness to real-world degradations remains underexplored. Blur, noise, low illumination, haze, and occlusion can obscure critical visual evidence, and such degradations are often heterogeneous and localized, so uniform restoration is both inefficient and potentially unnecessary.

We introduce ViRA, a Video Recovery Agent that integrates visual recovery directly into the reasoning process. Following a diagnose–restore–reason paradigm, ViRA jointly determines whether recovery is necessary, which restoration tools to invoke, and where in the video each tool should be applied. Conditioned on both the video and the query, it selectively recovers only the temporal regions whose degradation obscures task-relevant evidence, then reasons over the recovered observation. Across Video-MME, STAR, and NExT-QA, diverse degradation types, and multiple MLLM backbones, ViRA improves accuracy under corruption by 3.0 points on average and by up to 9.5 points over direct inference, while preserving, and even slightly improving, accuracy on uncorrupted videos.

02 — METHOD

Diagnose. Restore. Reason.

One decision stage. Given a degraded video and a query, the MLLM either answers directly, or emits a plan of temporally grounded tool calls u = (τ, [l, r]). Tools run in sequence on their frame ranges; everything outside stays untouched.

Try it: build a recovery plan

Each tile is one of the 16 sampled frames. Pick a corruption regime, then drag the tool-call bars (move them, or pull their edges) to choose where each tool runs. A frame clears only when the right tool covers it. Press ViRA plan to see the plan ViRA would emit for this layout.

The recovery toolbox

Ten degradation types in four families, each paired with one restorer. Every tool is a classical algorithm or a regression-trained network, never a generative model, so nothing can hallucinate evidence. Hover or tap a card to run its tool on the sample frame.
